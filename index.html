📲 Installing the App

1
Allow installs from unknown sources. On your Android phone, go to Settings → Apps → Special access → Install unknown apps, then enable it for your file manager or browser (whichever you'll use to open the APK).
2
Copy the APK to your phone. Transfer shivapriya-japa.apk to your phone via USB cable, email, Google Drive, or WhatsApp.
3
Open the APK. Tap the file on your phone and follow the prompts to install. If you're updating from an older version, uninstall the old one first so the new icon and audio settings refresh cleanly.
4
Launch. Look for the 🪔 icon in your app drawer.
Permissions: The app does not need internet, location, contacts, or microphone access. It runs entirely on your phone.

🚀 First-Time Setup

The app comes preloaded with common japas (Gayathri, Shiva, etc.). You can use them as-is or customize them. To add your own:

1
Open Settings (⚙️ icon, top-right).
2
Tap + Add Japa.
3
Enter the name in English and Kannada, set the time per cycle, and (optionally) attach an audio file.
4
Tap Save. The new japa will appear on the main screen.

📿 Daily Use

Starting a session

  • Tap any japa card to start its timer.
  • If audio is set, it plays automatically each cycle.
  • Tap again to pause.

Quick mute / unmute New

Each japa card on the main timer screen has a speaker icon in the top-right corner — 🔊 sound on or 🔇 muted. Tap it any time during a session to silence or restore the chant audio without leaving the timer screen. The icon is only shown for japas that have an audio file uploaded.

  • 🔊 → tap once to mute that japa. Audio stops immediately, count keeps running.
  • 🔇 → tap again to unmute. Audio resumes from where it left off.
  • The choice is remembered for next time and stays in sync with the Settings screen.

Resetting the count

  • Long-press the count, or use the reset button.
  • If you have a count in progress, the app asks for confirmation before clearing — so you don't lose progress by mistake.

Setting a target

You can set a target count (e.g., 108) for a japa. The app shows progress and notifies when you reach it.

🎵 Adding Audio (Nama / Mantra)

Each japa can have its own chant audio that plays once per cycle.

1
Open Settings → tap the japa you want to edit.
2
Tap Choose Audio and pick an MP3 or WAV from your phone.
3
The duration is detected automatically. Set the cycle time to match (or longer than) the audio length.
4
Use Speed to play it faster (1.2×, 1.5×) or slower (0.75×). Use Mute to silence it without removing it.
Tip: Keep audio files short (under 30 seconds) and small (under 1 MB) for best performance. Very large files take longer to load.

🌙 Keeping the Screen On

During longer japa sessions you don't want the phone screen to dim or lock. The app has a built-in toggle for this so you don't have to change phone-wide settings.

1
Open Settings (⚙️ tab at the bottom).
2
Find the 🌙 Display card near the top.
3
Turn on Keep screen on while app is running.
How it works: The screen stays on only while Japa Timer is open and visible. When you switch to another app or lock the phone, normal screen behavior resumes — so it won't drain battery in the background. The setting is remembered across launches.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The audio cuts off too early

Make sure the cycle time is at least as long as the audio length. If the audio is 6 seconds and you set the cycle to 3 seconds, the timer will start the next cycle before the audio finishes.

Audio pauses when I switch apps

Android pauses media when the app loses focus. Keep Japa Timer in the foreground for the audio to keep playing. (A future update may add background-audio support.)

Count didn't save after I closed the app

Counts save automatically as you go. If you reset and confirmed the dialog, the count was cleared — there's no way to recover a confirmed reset.

App icon looks wrong after update

Uninstall the old version completely, then install the new APK. Android sometimes caches the old icon during in-place updates.

Audio plays only after closing & reopening the app

This happens occasionally with very large audio files on first run. Pick a smaller file (under 1 MB) or compress your existing one with a free MP3 compressor.
